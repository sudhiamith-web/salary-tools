If you earn between ₹15,000 and ₹25,000 a month, your EPF deduction is about to change. The Employees' Provident Fund wage ceiling — frozen at ₹15,000 since 2014 — has just been raised to ₹25,000, and the new figure is already in effect. Here's exactly what changed, when, and what it means for your take-home pay.

### What actually changed

The Union Cabinet approved the increase on 16 September 2026, and the Ministry of Labour & Employment formally notified it the very next day through Gazette Notification S.O. 5109(E), issued under Section 2(89) of the Code on Social Security, 2020. The notification took effect from its date of publication — 17 September 2026 — and superseded an earlier notification, S.O. 2702(E), dated 29 May 2026.

The change is expected to bring more than 51 lakh additional employees within mandatory EPF coverage, extending provident fund, pension (EPS), and insurance (EDLI) protection to a wider band of the workforce.

### A ceiling frozen for over a decade

This is only the second revision to the EPF wage ceiling in over two decades. It stayed at ₹6,500 from 2004 to 2014, then rose to ₹15,000 in September 2014 — and stayed there for the next twelve years, even as average salaries climbed well past that figure. The ₹25,000 ceiling is meant to bring the statutory limit back in line with current wage levels.

| Period | Wage ceiling |
|---|---|
| 2004 – Sept 2014 | ₹6,500 |
| Sept 2014 – 16 Sept 2026 | ₹15,000 |
| From 17 Sept 2026 | ₹25,000 |

### What the ceiling actually controls

It's worth being precise about what this number does. The wage ceiling isn't a maximum salary — it's the wage figure used to calculate the *statutory minimum* EPF/EPS contribution. Contribution rates haven't changed at all: employees still contribute 12% of their PF wages, and employers still contribute 12% in total, split between the EPS pension fund (8.33%) and the EPF account (the remaining 3.67%).

What changes is the wage figure that 12% is calculated on — for employees whose contribution was capped at ₹15,000, it can now go up to ₹25,000.

**A worked example.** Take an employee with actual monthly wages of ₹20,000, contributing on the capped (statutory minimum) basis:

| | Old ceiling (₹15,000) | New ceiling (₹25,000) |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution wage | ₹15,000 (capped) | ₹20,000 (below ceiling, so actual wage applies) |
| Employee PF (12%) | ₹1,800 | ₹2,400 |
| Employer EPS (8.33%) | ₹1,250 | ₹1,666 |
| Employer EPF residual | ₹550 | ₹734 |
| **Total monthly flow** | **₹3,600** | **₹4,800** |

For an employee already earning ₹25,000 or more, the contribution wage simply moves from a ₹15,000 cap to a ₹25,000 cap — employee PF rises from ₹1,800 to ₹3,000 a month, purely from the ceiling change.

### The September 2026 transition month, explained

Here's where it gets genuinely complicated, and where most explainers oversimplify. The new ceiling took effect on 17 September — in the middle of the month. That means September 2026 straddles two different ceilings: the old ₹15,000 ceiling for 1–16 September, and the new ₹25,000 ceiling for 17–30 September.

The Gazette notification itself says nothing about how to handle this broken period. In practice, employers are choosing between two approaches while they wait for an EPFO circular to formally address it:

- **Pro-rating**, where the contribution wage for the month is a days-weighted blend of the ₹15,000 cap for the first 16 days and the ₹25,000 cap for the remaining 14 days.
- **Not pro-rating**, where the new ₹25,000 ceiling is simply applied to the whole month, on the reasoning that splitting a statutory change mid-month adds complexity without clear regulatory backing either way.

Neither is currently confirmed as the officially mandated method. If your payroll team hasn't settled on one yet, that's a normal position to be in right now — just be consistent about whichever one you pick, and revisit it if EPFO issues specific guidance.

### Who's newly covered

Employees already earning ₹15,000 or less were already within mandatory coverage, and nothing changes for them. The employees most affected are those earning between ₹15,001 and ₹25,000 a month:

- **Existing PF members** capped at ₹15,000 will generally see their contribution base rise toward their actual wages, up to ₹25,000.
- **Employees already contributing on actual (uncapped) wages** under an existing higher-wage arrangement won't see their total contribution change, though the employer's internal split between EPS and EPF shifts slightly.
- **Employees who were never PF members** because their wages exceeded the ceiling in force when they joined are a genuinely case-specific question — whether they now require fresh enrolment depends on details EPFO hasn't fully clarified yet. This is worth confirming directly rather than assuming either way.

Employees earning above ₹25,000 who were never PF members remain outside mandatory coverage, exactly as before.

### What employers should do now

- Update the PF ceiling parameter in payroll/HRMS systems from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000, effective 17 September 2026.
- Decide and document your September transition approach (pro-rated or not) and apply it consistently.
- Re-check that EPS still calculates on the capped wage by default — only an employee with an already-exercised higher-pension option should have EPS running on actual wages.
- Recompute CTC structures where employer PF is built into CTC, since the higher employer contribution for affected employees can shift net take-home if CTC isn't revisited.
- Communicate the change clearly to affected employees before their next payslip lands.

1. **Q:** When exactly did the ₹25,000 EPF ceiling take effect?
   **A:** 17 September 2026 — the date the Gazette notification (S.O. 5109(E)) was published. It applies from that date, with no retrospective effect.

2. **Q:** Did my PF contribution rate change, or just the ceiling?
   **A:** Just the ceiling. You still contribute 12% of your PF wages; the employer still contributes 12% in total. Only the wage figure that percentage is calculated on has a higher cap now.

3. **Q:** Do I now contribute on ₹25,000 even though I earn more than that?
   **A:** No. ₹25,000 is a ceiling, not a target. If your actual wages are below it, contributions on the capped basis use your actual wages. If they're above it, contributions on the capped basis are capped at ₹25,000 — unless your employer already contributes on your actual, uncapped wages under an existing arrangement.

4. **Q:** Is my EPS (pension) contribution also based on ₹25,000 now?
   **A:** By default, yes — EPS stays capped at the ceiling even when your PF wage basis is uncapped. It only runs on actual wages if you already have a valid, exercised higher-pension option in place, which is uncommon.

5. **Q:** How is PF calculated for September 2026, since the change fell mid-month?
   **A:** There's no official methodology yet — see the transition-month section above. Use the calculator to see both the pro-rated and not-pro-rated results side by side.
